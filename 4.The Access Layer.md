# Access Layer — What I Understood

The **Access Layer** is where end devices such as PCs, printers, and phones connect to the network. Switches are commonly used here to provide these connections.

What I understood is that the access layer is not just about connecting cables. The switch also controls how devices communicate within the local network.

### Key points I learned

* End devices normally connect to **switch ports** at the access layer.
* A switch learns the **MAC addresses** of connected devices and stores them in its MAC address table.
* **VLANs** can be used to separate devices into different logical networks even when they use the same physical switch.
* Devices in different VLANs cannot communicate directly through a Layer 2 switch.
* Communication between different VLANs requires a **Layer 3 device**, such as a router or Layer 3 switch.
* The access layer can therefore be used to control which devices belong to the same local network.

### What I observed in Packet Tracer

When I placed PCs into different VLANs, devices in the same VLAN could communicate, while devices in different VLANs could not communicate through the switch alone.

This helped me understand that a switch does more than simply forward packets — **VLAN configuration can be used to separate network traffic and create boundaries between groups of devices.**
